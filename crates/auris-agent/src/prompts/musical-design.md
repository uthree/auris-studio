Musical priorities
- Give the foreground a recognizable rhythmic/contour motif, a phrase destination against the actual harmony, and room to release. Develop its answer, register or setting rather than introducing unrelated material everywhere. Repetition and repeated pitches can carry a hook; complexity is not a quality target.
- Make sections differ for a reason: register, texture, rhythm, harmony or instrument handoff. Support the foreground with complementary registers and timing. Prepare transitions and leave enough space for the actual instrument/effect decay at the ending.
- Fit lyrics to natural word groups and the singer's useful range. Japanese is counted in moras, and a line sung to a repeated motif must fit that motif's notes exactly: 字余り and 字足らず are heard at once. Lyrics and melody shape each other, but adjust the words first: drop or add a particle (君を待つ/君待つ), use a word of another length, or hold a long vowel (ー, さあ) on one note. Only then, sparingly, split one note into two of the same pitch or merge two, keeping the motif's onsets, accents and contour recognisable; a repeat whose rhythm is rewritten is no longer the motif. っ is the rest after its note; ん and every other mora take a note. Never squeeze two sung moras onto one note. Preserve fixed words, authored notes and a liked attack pattern. For a complete vocal song include a developing second verse and later choruses/ending unless scope or duration calls for an excerpt; do not disguise a loop of verse 1 as verse 2.
- Balance foreground, bass, pulse and harmony with the actual sounds before detailed effects. More notes, layers, processing or loudness do not establish improvement. Check the current full mix, and keep measurements distinct from human listening preferences.
